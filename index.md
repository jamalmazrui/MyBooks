## About these books {#about-these-books}

I am a blind author, software developer and accessibility professional, and these are the 22 books I have published on Amazon's Kindle store. Most are narrative nonfiction: true stories and verified facts, told plainly. The rest are practical guides on accessibility and on publishing, and two are government documents I prepared for Kindle so that they could be read with a screen reader.

Some habits run through them. Each book is built to be read with a screen reader, by speech or braille, as comfortably as with eyes: real headings, a linked table of contents and no information carried by pictures alone. Each book names its sources, and the Strange Truths series checks every story against peer-reviewed research, official reports, memoirs and reference works. For readability, many of the books are written at about a ninth-grade reading level, with technical terms explained the first time they appear. And AI models have assisted the writing of many of the books; each one that had such help says so in its own pages.

Each title below is a link to the book's page on Amazon. The description under it is a short version of the one on that page. Eighteen of these books, the ones I wrote alone, also appear with books by other blind and low-vision authors in [Blind Books][blind-books].

## Getting an EPUB copy {#getting-an-epub-copy}

All of these books are published without copy protection, called DRM, and that choice is confirmed with Amazon for every title. Since January 2026, Amazon lets a reader who has bought a book without DRM download it as an EPUB or PDF file, which opens in any reading app, on any device, with no Amazon software needed. For some readers an EPUB in the reading app of their choice is more accessible than the Kindle app, so here is how to get it, with a keyboard and a screen reader.

1. Buy the book from its link below, signed in to your Amazon account. The download is offered only to a buyer of the book, not to a Kindle Unlimited borrower, and it appears a day or three after the book is published or updated.
2. Open [Manage Your Content and Devices][mycd] on Amazon's site, signed in to the same account. It is also reachable from Amazon's home page: open the Account & Lists menu and choose Content & Devices. The page opens on the Content tab with your books listed.
3. Find the book. Press the key that moves by heading until you hear its title, or type the title into the search box above the list and press Enter.
4. On the book's row, Tab to the button that opens its menu of actions, named something like More actions, and press Enter or Space. A list of actions opens.
5. Arrow down to Download EPUB or Download PDF and press Enter. The browser saves the file to your Downloads folder, and announces the download in the way it usually does.
6. Open the file in any EPUB reader. Free ones that work well with screen readers include Paperback and Thorium Reader on Windows and Mac, and Apple Books on iPhone and iPad.

Amazon's own description of the feature, written for publishers, is on the [KDP page about DRM][kdp-drm]. If a download is not offered for one of these books, write to me and I will check the setting on my side.

## Ali Mazrui {#ali-mazrui}

Two books about Ali A. Mazrui (1933–2014), the Kenyan-born political scientist whose writing and lectures on Africa, Islam and world affairs reached a worldwide audience.

### [Legacy of Mazrui][legacy] {#legacy-of-mazrui}

Enduring Esteem for the Man and His Work. A chronicle of the first ten years after his death: the memorial lectures, research centers, awards, publications, archives and renamed streets through which his work has been remembered on several continents.

### [The Maxims of Mazrui][maxims] {#the-maxims-of-mazrui}

Life Lessons from a Wise Professor. A collection of the professor's maxims, each a lesson in a sentence or two.

## Among the Greatest {#among-the-greatest}

Four books that each ask who, or what, stands among the greatest in a field, and answer with profiles rather than rankings. In each, no person or work appears twice, so as many as possible get their due. The choices in the tennis and music books were made with the help of OpenAI's GPT-4 in 2024, under my direction.

### [Mastery in Men's Tennis][tennis] {#mastery-in-mens-tennis}

Among the Greatest Strokes of All Time. For each of 23 strokes of the open era, from the one-handed slice backhand to the overhead smash, one player is named as its greatest exponent, with how he hit it and the matches where it mattered.

### [Mastery in Music Genre][music-genre] {#mastery-in-music-genre}

Among the Greatest Albums of All Time. One hundred albums of songs in English, ten in each of ten genres from blues to vocal jazz, with what each album is, when it came out and why it lasts. Offered as a listening guide, not a verdict.

### [Mastery in Nonvisual Accomplishment][nonvisual] {#mastery-in-nonvisual-accomplishment}

Among the Greatest Blind Persons of All Time. Profiles of 100 people across 20 fields, from mathematics and science to music, government, religion, literature and technology, who achieved greatness without sight.

### [Mastery in Popular Song][popular-song] {#mastery-in-popular-song}

Among the Greatest Musicians of All Time. Two hundred musicians across ten roles, from lead voice and lyrics to bass guitar and drums, and ten genres, a woman and a man in each pairing, with what each contributed to English-language popular song.

## Strange Truths {#strange-truths}

Ten volumes of verified true stories about the world, each with 26 chapters, or 16 in the case of Original Senses, that stand alone and can be read in any order, plus one volume that collects all ten. Each book ends with key terms, timelines and further reading.

### [Strange Truths][strange-truths-book] {#strange-truths-collected}

Objective Stories that Challenge Our Understanding of the World. The collected edition: all ten volumes, 229 chapters, in one reading sequence. A reader who wants the whole series needs only this book.

### [Captivating Numbers][numbers] {#captivating-numbers}

26 Intriguing Numbers Discovered in Mathematics and Science. From pi and e to Graham's number and Chaitin's Omega, the constants that shape mathematics and physics, and the people who found them.

### [The Edges of Maps][edges] {#the-edges-of-maps}

Understanding and Managing the Limits of Human Knowledge. Five kinds of limit on what anyone can ever know, in physics, mathematics, computation and the mind, and how to live and decide well within them.

### [Enduring Enigmas][enigmas] {#enduring-enigmas}

26 Unresolved Mysteries in World History. Questions of history, forensics and archaeology on which the evidence stops just short of certainty, each examined with the same care for what is known and what is not.

### [Mental Marvels][marvels] {#mental-marvels}

26 Remarkable Feats of the Human Mind. Documented extremes of memory, calculation and perception, from a deck of cards memorized in under fourteen seconds to a grandmaster playing 48 games blindfolded, arranged across 1,400 years.

### [Mortal Ventures][ventures] {#mortal-ventures}

26 Daring Demonstrations of Human Capacity. Deliberate, verified feats that succeeded, from a rope crossing of Niagara Gorge in 1859 to record free dives, with no tragedies and no failed attempts.

### [Original Senses][senses] {#original-senses}

The Wondrous Ways that Life Senses the World. Sixteen sensory systems that life has evolved, from echolocation in bats and electroreception in sharks to the magnetic compass of sea turtles, showing how far past five senses nature has gone.

### [Persistent Paradoxes][paradoxes] {#persistent-paradoxes}

26 Challenges to Human Reason Across the Ages. From Zeno's race to the black hole information paradox, puzzles that produced calculus, set theory and the Big Bang model when they were resolved, and that remain open where they were not.

### [Returning Alive][returning] {#returning-alive}

26 Amazing Stories of Human Survival. Two centuries of true survival stories, each verified in memoirs, accident reports, research and journalism, with what each survivor did to come home.

### [Uncanny Creatures][creatures] {#uncanny-creatures}

26 Astonishing Forms of Life. A salamander that regrows its legs, a bacterium that shrugs off radiation, a rootless five-foot flower that smells of rotting meat, and other organisms improbably shaped by evolution.

### [Unsettled Universe][universe] {#unsettled-universe}

26 Baffling Anomalies in Modern Cosmology. The open puzzles of astronomy and cosmology that sit beside what we know for certain about a 13.8-billion-year-old universe.

## Other books {#other-books}

Three practical books on accessibility, authoring and publishing.

### [Blind Vibe Coding][vibe-coding] {#blind-vibe-coding}

Building Apps Nonvisually with AI. How people who are blind or have low vision can build their own apps with AI coding tools, a keyboard and a screen reader, drawn from building a family of Windows programs that way in 2026. Published in October 2026.

### [Bringing a Book from Mind to Market][mind-to-market] {#bringing-a-book-from-mind-to-market}

Accessibility, Authoring, and Self-Publishing Through Amazon. The story of publishing my first Kindle book without sight, step by step from manuscript and cover to pricing and promotion, with the accessibility problems met at each stage and the solutions that worked, for blind writers and for the people who make their tools.

### [WCAG in a Book][wcag] {#wcag-in-a-book}

Understanding the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines. All 87 success criteria of WCAG 2.2 in numbered order, each paired with the W3C's full Understanding document, followed by overviews of the wider W3C accessibility standards such as WAI-ARIA, ATAG, UAAG and WCAG2ICT.

## Public documents as Kindle editions {#public-documents-as-kindle-editions}

Two United States government documents that I prepared as Kindle editions because none existed, so that they could be read with a screen reader with real headings and linked navigation. I did not write them; the text is the agency's own, in the public domain, and the agency is the author on Amazon.

### [Individuals with Cognitive Disabilities][cognitive] {#individuals-with-cognitive-disabilities}

Barriers to and Solutions for Accessible Information and Communication Technologies. The Federal Communications Commission's 2016 white paper on how people with cognitive disabilities use, and are kept from using, information and communication technology.

### [Information and Communication Technology (ICT) Standards and Guidelines][ict] {#ict-standards-and-guidelines}

The U.S. Access Board's 2017 rule that refreshed the Section 508 standards for federal technology and the Section 255 guidelines for telecommunications equipment.
